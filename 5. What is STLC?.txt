So, if the SDLC is the blueprint for building software, the Software Testing Life Cycle (STLC) is the blueprint for testing that software. It outlines the entire process of testing, from the initial planning to the final release. The STLC ensures that quality checks are carried out systematically and that no bugs slip through the cracks. It’s not just about testing; it’s about knowing when to test and how to test at each stage of the project. It’s like a series of checkpoints where you ensure the product is on track and meeting requirements. The key stages of STLC include test planning, test case design, test execution, defect reporting and test closure. As a QA engineer, you will be involved in each of these stages, and understanding them  will make your testing efforts more efficient and organized.
